VAT in the Gulf: how it affects the price you pay

VAT rates in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain and Oman, why Kuwait and Qatar don’t charge it, and how it affects discounts and orders from abroad.

Updated: 9 November 20263 min readBy the Kobon team

The short version

  • Standard VAT rates: Saudi Arabia 15%, Bahrain 10%, UAE 5%, Oman 5%.
  • Kuwait and Qatar haven’t introduced VAT.
  • VAT applies to imports too, even when a parcel is exempt from customs duty.
  • Code discounts are usually shown against VAT-inclusive prices, so check the order summary for the real total.

What VAT is

Value added tax is a consumption tax charged on most goods and services at each stage of sale, and ultimately paid by the shopper as part of the price. GCC states agreed a common framework, but each country chose its own timing and rate, which is why the same product can end up costing different amounts across the Gulf.

Rates across the Gulf

CountryStandard rate
Saudi Arabia15%
Bahrain10%
UAE5%
Oman5%
KuwaitNot introduced
QatarNot introduced

These are standard rates. Some goods and services are exempt or zero-rated under each country’s rules, and rates can change by government decision, so check your national tax authority if it matters.

Is the price VAT-inclusive?

Stores selling to consumers inside a country normally show prices including VAT. International stores may show a price without import VAT, adding it at checkout or on delivery. Check the order summary before you pay and look for “VAT included” or “duties and taxes included”.

VAT on orders from abroad

This is the bit people miss: a parcel exempt from customs duty isn’t exempt from VAT. In Saudi Arabia, ZATCA says VAT is collected on imports regardless of the shipment’s value, except where the VAT rules exclude it, and is calculated on the goods, shipping, insurance and fees. The UAE likewise charges VAT on imported goods. Always add VAT when comparing an international store with a local one.

How VAT interacts with a discount code

Typically the code comes off the item price and VAT is calculated on the discounted amount, so the final price drops by roughly the same percentage. Stores display this differently, though. What matters is the “total due” in the order summary, not the percentage on the code.

Example in Saudi Arabia: an item costs SAR 230 including VAT. A 10% code brings it to SAR 207 including VAT. You save SAR 23 in total, part of which would have gone to tax.

Is it cheaper to buy from another Gulf country?

A product can look cheaper on a store in a lower-VAT country, but a cross-border order may be taxed in the country where you receive it, on top of shipping and clearance. In most cases it’s simpler and clearer to buy from the store’s version for your own country, where the price, warranty and returns are designed for you.

Practical tips

  • Compare final prices including VAT and shipping, not pre-tax prices.
  • Ask for a tax invoice and keep it. You’ll need it for warranty claims and returns.
  • Watch for pre-VAT prices on stores aimed at businesses.
  • On international orders, prepay duties and taxes when offered, so you know the full cost up front.

This guide is general information, not tax advice. Your national tax authority has the final word.

Common questions

Do I pay VAT on shipping?

On imports, VAT is calculated on the goods including shipping, insurance and fees, as ZATCA explains for Saudi Arabia. On local orders, delivery charges are normally treated as part of the supply.

Can I ask for a tax invoice?

Yes. VAT-registered stores issue invoices showing the VAT. Keep them as proof of what you paid, for warranty claims and returns.

Is VAT refunded when I return something?

For a local return you’re normally refunded the full amount including VAT. Import VAT on overseas orders can be harder to recover, so check the store’s policy.
